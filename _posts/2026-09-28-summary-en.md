**「Background」** AI Safety researcher Taras Kutsyk describes a recurring failure mode in LLM-assisted research: agents either produce unusable results in hands-off mode or drain the researcher in hands-on mode. **「Solution」** Kutsyk proposes Sector, a four-level agentic workflow that prioritizes clarity, verifiability, traceability, and active human engagement. Level 1 defines &\#x27;what good looks like&\#x27; upfront to align evaluation criteria. Level 3 automates book-keeping by linking every report to its exact config, commit ID, and output location, while branch cards track workstream progress, established findings, and dead ends to prevent retrying failed approaches. Level 4 keeps researchers engaged through game-like activities such as &\#x27;Sabotage Hunt,&\#x27; where users find errors planted in walkthroughs, and &\#x27;Queen&\#x27;s Move,&\#x27; where researchers write key code components themselves while the agent builds around them. The system is modular and opt-in; researchers can use just one level or engage deeply, with a SECTOR-OFF flag allowing temporary bypass during rushes. Kutsyk reports highest confidence in Levels 2 and 3, moderate confidence in Level 1, and acknowledges Level 4 remains the least tested.

**「Takeaway」** Sector reframes LLM-assisted research from a lottery into a transparent, controllable process where human oversight is structurally preserved through verifiable traceability, multi-round planning, and gamified engagement rather than manual micromanagement.
