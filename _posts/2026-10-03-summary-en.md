### [Longitudinal AI-assisted self-audit of communication patterns](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PAzerqAofFCLoWsgK/reviewing-my-lesswrong-post-history-with-claude) ⭐️ 3.0/10

rss · LessWrong AI风险社区 · Oct 3, 01:42

**「Background」** The author used Claude Code to audit seven years of posts and comments on LessWrong and the EA Forum, plus nearly ten thousand Lichess blitz games, to identify persistent cognitive biases and communication failures. The motivation was personal accountability and testing whether AI could surf longitudinal personal data to reveal hidden behavioral loops that are difficult to see in isolation.

**「Solution」** Claude Code analyzed semantic tone, hedging language, and downvote rates across time. The audit revealed that most low-scoring comments arose from a single cycle: making a loose claim, facing correction, then defending by narrowing or attributing motive. The author identified nine specific flaws, including strawmanning, absolutist framing, and asymmetrical rigor demands. Crucially, the data showed net-negative karma rates increased sixfold in 2025–2026, coinciding with decreased hedging and increased contempt. Cross-referencing chess ratings provided an independent cognitive signal: rating dips correlated with periods of extreme stress, isolation, and poor sleep during a demanding MS and cross-country move. The analysis suggested the decline was not due to site culture changes or general cognitive aging, but rather stress-induced conflict orientation and a feedback loop where isolation drives engagement-seeking, which triggers negative reception when the author is exhausted.

**「Takeaway」** Stress and exhaustion reliably predict conflict-oriented communication and degraded intellectual engagement. The author concludes that self-regulation of stress, isolation, and sleep is a prerequisite for productive online discourse, not merely a wellness tip.
